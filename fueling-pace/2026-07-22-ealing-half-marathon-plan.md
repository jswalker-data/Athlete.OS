### Estimates (flagged, with how to get the real number)

- **Sweat rate: ~0.75 L/hr (ESTIMATE).** Built from Josh's light body
  weight and this race's cool, dry conditions, nudged up slightly for
  self-reported heat sensitivity and a hard race effort. *Measure it for
  real:* weigh yourself (same kit, bathroom empty) immediately before and
  after a run of 60+ minutes with no bathroom break mid-run — `(pre-weight
  kg − post-weight kg + any fluid drunk in L) ÷ hours run = sweat rate
  L/hr`.
- **Sweat sodium: ~600 mg/L (ESTIMATE, low end of the population range).**
  Set low because Josh reports no salty-sweater signs (no visible salt
  stains, sweat doesn't sting or taste strongly salty). *Measure it for
  real:* not essential at this race distance/risk level, but a sweat-sodium
  patch test (or just tracking whether salt crusts visibly on kit over
  several long runs) would sharpen this if he moves to longer or hotter
  races later.

## 2. Fueling plan

### Carbs — 2 gels, ~29 g/hr

This race's duration (1.7 hr) sits in the IOC's 1-2.5hr bracket, which
recommends 30-60 g/hr. **That's not what's prescribed here** — the binding
constraint isn't oxidation capacity, it's that Josh's gut has never
processed a gel. Debuting a new fueling product at target rate on race day
is a classic way to manufacture a GI problem that wasn't there before.

- **2 standard gels (~25g carbohydrate each) = 50g total ÷ 1.7 hr ≈ 29 g/hr.**
- This is deliberately below the 30-60 g/hr target. If gel practice on
  training long runs (see below) goes well, a 3rd gel could be added for
  future races (~44g/hr) — not this one, not without testing first.
- Standard single-source gels are fine at this rate — no need for a
  glucose:fructose blend, that only matters above ~60g/hr.

### Fluid — ~0.5 L/hr, carry a flask

- Estimated sweat loss: 0.75 L/hr × 1.7 hr ≈ **1.28 L total** over the race.
- As a fraction of body weight: 1.28 L ÷ 61 kg ≈ **2.1% body mass** — right
  at the ACSM 2% threshold where under-replacing starts to cost
  performance. **Flag.**
- Target replacement: ~65% of sweat loss during the race (full replacement
  isn't necessary for a sub-2-hour effort and the rest is made up
  post-race) → 0.75 × 0.65 ≈ **0.5 L/hr**, well under the ~1.0-1.2 L/hr gut
  absorption ceiling, so this is comfortably drinkable.
- **Course water alone won't deliver this evenly.** Per the dossier, the
  only on-course water is at mile 4, mile 5.5, mid-mile 9, and the finish —
  that's a 31-minute dry stretch from the gun to mile 4, and a **32-minute
  dry stretch from mile 9 to the finish**, which is exactly where the
  course's known mile 10-11 fade risk lives. Recommend carrying a small
  handheld or belt flask (~500ml) rather than relying on 3 cups over 102
  minutes, especially given the heat-sensitivity self-report.

### Sodium — no separate product needed, one contingency

- Estimated loss: 0.75 L/hr × 600 mg/L ≈ 450 mg/hr → **~765 mg total** over
  the race.
- The 2 gels contribute roughly 120mg sodium (~60mg each, typical for a
  standard gel) — leaving a ~645mg gap unreplaced during the race.
- **This gap is low-risk, not a flag.** Non-salty-sweater self-report, cool
  conditions, and a sub-2-hour race are exactly the profile where sports
  nutrition guidance treats in-race sodium replacement as low priority.
- **Contingency only:** if race morning turns out warmer than the
  dossier's historical pattern, drop an electrolyte tab in the flask —
  cheap insurance given Josh's self-reported heat sensitivity, no downside.

## 3. Pacing plan

**Flat target:** 7:47/mi. **Altitude:** sea level, not a factor — no
adjustment applied.

**Elevation:** the dossier itself couldn't extract a reliable mile-by-mile
elevation dataset (~95-140m total, "rolling," no severe named climb, two
sources disagree on the exact number). Rather than fabricate precise
grade-adjusted splits from data that doesn't exist, the call here is
**effort over pace on any incline** — ease off slightly on climbs, let it
back on descents, using the Minetti cost curve directionally (a mild grade
like this course costs low-single-digit percent extra effort, nothing
that needs a rewritten pace table).

- **Miles 1-2: 7:55-8:05/mi, deliberately slower than goal pace.** This is
  the dossier's #1 named risk at this specific race — crowd energy and
  pacers pulling runners out too fast, fading hard by mile 10-11. Two more
  reasons to bank nothing early this time: an untested fueling product and
  self-reported heat sensitivity both make an early deficit more expensive
  to pay back later.
- **Miles 3-9: 7:47/mi goal pace**, held by effort through the mild rolling
  sections rather than chased on the watch.
- **Miles 10-13.1: this is the flagged zone.** Hold effort, not the split.
  If pace drifts to 7:55-8:05/mi here on a legitimately hard effort, that's
  the plan working — not a failure to be sprinted back. Gel 2 and the
  flask are positioned right before this stretch on purpose. Expect it to
  feel harder even if the numbers hold, especially if it's warmed up by
  then per the dossier's finisher-report pattern.

## 4. Flags

1. **Untested product on race day — the biggest risk in this plan.** Carb
   rate is set to 29 g/hr specifically because of this, well under the
   30-60 g/hr this duration would otherwise support. Non-negotiable: test
   gels in training before relying on them here (see below).
2. **Estimated body-mass loss (~2.1%) sits right at the ACSM 2%
   threshold** — this is why a flask is recommended over the sparse
   3-stop course water.
3. **Sweat rate and sweat sodium are both estimates**, not measured — see
   the how-to-measure notes in Section 1 if Josh wants to tighten these.
4. **Heat tolerance is self-reported**, and this race's historical
   conditions are mild — the plan doesn't over-correct, but "effort not
   pace" in the final miles is the built-in insurance.
5. **No reliable per-mile elevation data exists** for this course — the
   pacing plan uses effort-based guidance on rolling sections rather than
   invented precision.

## 5. Before race day

- **Test gels on 2-3 upcoming long runs**, using the exact product planned
  for race day, at roughly this rate (one gel around 30-40 min in, a
  second later in the run). This is also called for directly in the race
  dossier's "what this means I need to train" list (item 4) — this plan
  and that one agree on the same gap.
- **Get a small handheld or belt flask** before race day if Josh doesn't
  have one — the course water spacing doesn't cover the fluid target
  without it.
- **Optional but useful:** a real sweat-rate test on one long run (see
  Section 1) would replace the biggest estimate in this plan with a real
  number.

If any of these inputs change — a measured sweat rate comes back, gel
practice goes badly and the carb rate needs to drop further, goal time
gets revised — re-run `/fuel-and-pace` rather than hand-editing this file;
the recomputed numbers should be shown, not patched in isolation.
