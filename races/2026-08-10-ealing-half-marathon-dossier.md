## 1. The course (unchanged since Jul 22)

- **Format:** 13.1mi / 21.1km, single lap, fully road-closed. ([ealinghalfmarathon.com](https://ealinghalfmarathon.com/))
- **Start/finish:** Walpole Park, Ealing (since the 2023 route change). Pre-2023 finisher reports describe a different Lammas Park start — historically useful, not literally current. ([route page](https://ealinghalfmarathon.com/route-2/), [Ealing.News 2024 guide](https://www.ealing.news/sport/essential-guide-for-running-or-watching-the-ealing-half-marathon-2024/))
- **Route:** Central Ealing → Montpelier → Pitshanger → West Ealing → Hanwell → St Stephen's → back to Walpole Park. Several roads passed **twice** (Uxbridge Rd, Pitshanger Ln, Drayton Bridge Rd, Cuckoo Ave) — expect crowding at those pinch points. ([Ealing.News](https://www.ealing.news/sport/essential-guide-for-running-or-watching-the-ealing-half-marathon-2024/))
- **Elevation — sources still disagree:** ~140m gain "rolling" ([goodrunguide](https://www.goodrunguide.co.uk/ElevationGraph.asp?RunType=S&RunID=299129)) vs ~95m "mostly flat" (aggregator). Either way: mild-to-moderate undulation, no signature climb, 100% paved. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ealing_Half_Marathon))
- **Aid (2024 edition, confirm nearer date):** water ~mi 4 / 5.5 / 9 / finish; oranges ~mi 7.5; first aid near mi 6 and 9-10.

## 2. Conditions (unchanged)

Late-Sept London, 9am start: cool/cold start (~10-13°C), dry more often than not, **warming noticeably by the back half** per multiple finisher reports — dress to shed a layer, don't overdress. Low humidity, ~10-11mph mostly-westerly wind, rain chance ~26% by month end. Re-check the forecast in race week. ([WeatherSpark Heathrow Sept](https://weatherspark.com/m/147876/9/Average-Weather-in-September-at-London-Heathrow-Airport-United-Kingdom), [Eat Cake and Run 2024](https://eatcakeandrun.uk/2024/09/29/race-report-ealing-half-marathon-2024/), [2025](https://eatcakeandrun.uk/2025/09/28/race-report-ealing-half-marathon-25/))

## 3. What finishers warn about (unchanged — and now Josh's #1 worry)

**The mile 10-11 fade, named independently across years and levels:** first-timers transitioning to walk-running at mile 10 ([Running on Full](https://runningonfullblog.com/race-review-ealing-half/)); "the 11th mile... I could barely feel my legs" ([Redeemer London 2019](https://redeemerlondon.org/blog/2019/10/1/ealing-half-marathon-a-review)); a 2025 finisher going down hard ~mile 11 after clinging to faster pacers, self-diagnosed as "hubris" ([Eat Cake and Run 2025](https://eatcakeandrun.uk/2025/09/28/race-report-ealing-half-marathon-25/)). Not terrain — it's the classic **go-out-too-hot fade**, amplified by this race's *enormous* crowd (30,000+ spectators, drumming, jelly babies) that repeatedly pulls runners past their real pace early. This is the exact risk Josh named as his top worry (§6).

## 4. Gear & logistics (unchanged)

9:00am start, Walpole Park. Bag drop in the race village (max 40×30×10cm). No event parking — use Northfields (~10min walk) or Ealing Broadway (~15min). Confirm 2026 packet-pickup times nearer the date. Gear: ditchable start layer, sunscreen even if overcast, nothing trail-specific. Full detail in the Jul 22 dossier.

**Biggest worry now: pacing — going out too hot.** With the long-run gap closing (his old #1 worry), Josh's concern has shifted to exactly the failure mode the finisher reports warn about most: getting swept out too fast by the crowd/pacers and fading at mile 10-11. **Target held: 1:42:00** — re-confirmed. The honest projection is ~1:51 expected with 1:42 at ~15% (see time-prediction), so 1:42 is a genuine stretch — but Josh wants to train for it, and the rewritten plan builds toward it with paces set off 7:47/mi.

## 7. What this means I need to train (rewritten, pacing-led)

1. **Race-pace discipline is the spine.** Rehearse 7:47/mi feel in small doses from Phase 2, growing into bigger race-pace blocks in the taper. Long runs finish controlled/negative-split, never fading. This directly answers the top worry.
2. **Parkruns become pacing labs, not blowouts** — run even or negative split (leash is off, but "hard" ≠ "reckless start"). This trains the exact discipline the race demands.
3. **Extend the long run to ~11.5mi peak** on the ≤10%/week guardrail off the rolling 3-week max — closing the endurance tax that gates 1:42.
4. **Hold true Z2 at mile 8-10 effort on long runs**, not just early miles — the aerobic-efficiency work has to hold deep into the run.
5. **Rehearse race-morning fueling** on the 9mi+ long runs (~30-60g carbs/hr) before race day.
6. **Route 1-2 long runs on rolling terrain**; a conservative, pre-decided opening-2-miles plan by HR for race day.

## 8. A / B / C goals (2026-08-10)

- **A — sub-1:40:00:** upside case, only if the long-run ramp *and* the aerobic/pacing gaps all close cleanly with no setback.
- **B — 1:42:00:** the held target. A stretch on today's data (~15% likelihood), but the plan is built to chase it with disciplined pacing.
- **C — finish injury-free, well-paced:** the floor, non-negotiable given the knee/hip history — outranks time on the day if the two conflict. Given the top worry is pacing, "well-paced" is folded into the floor: a controlled race that fades gracefully beats a hot start that blows up at mile 11.
