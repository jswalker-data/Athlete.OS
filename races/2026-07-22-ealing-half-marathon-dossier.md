## 1. The course

**Format:** 13.1 miles / 21.1km, single lap, fully road-closed — "West
London's only fully road-closed half marathon."
([ealinghalfmarathon.com](https://ealinghalfmarathon.com/))

**Start/finish:** Since a 2023 route change (works in Lammas Park moved
the finish), the race starts and finishes at **Walpole Park**, Ealing —
the 2024 edition's start point was Culmington Road with a Walpole Park
finish. **Older race reports (2012-2019) describe the pre-2023 route**
(Lammas Park start), so treat their course-feel comments as historically
informative but not literally current.
([ealinghalfmarathon.com](https://ealinghalfmarathon.com/route-2/),
[Ealing.News essential guide 2024](https://www.ealing.news/sport/essential-guide-for-running-or-watching-the-ealing-half-marathon-2024/))

**Route:** Central Ealing → Montpelier → Pitshanger → West Ealing →
Hanwell → St Stephen's, back to Walpole Park. Some roads are passed
**twice** (Uxbridge Road, Pitshanger Lane, Drayton Bridge Road, Cuckoo
Avenue are named as double-pass spectator points) — expect real
congestion at those pinch points, not a clean single-direction loop
throughout.
([Ealing.News](https://www.ealing.news/sport/essential-guide-for-running-or-watching-the-ealing-half-marathon-2024/))

**Elevation — sources disagree, reporting both:**
- ~140m total gain, described as "rolling" ([goodrunguide.co.uk hill
  profile](https://www.goodrunguide.co.uk/ElevationGraph.asp?RunType=S&RunID=299129)
  page confirms a hill profile exists for this course but its
  numeric/graph data wasn't extractable from text)
- ~95m / 312ft, described as "mostly flat" (secondary aggregator source)

No source names a single severe or "signature" climb — this is not a
hilly course by UK half-marathon standards, it's mild-to-moderate
undulation throughout. Surface is 100% paved road, no technical or
off-road sections.

**Course records:** Men 1:04:15 (Josphat Kemei, 2016); Women 1:14:46
(Teresiah Omosa, 2016). Founded 2012, cancelled only in 2020 (COVID).
Voted UK's Best Half Marathon three years running by the Running Awards.
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ealing_Half_Marathon))

**Aid/support on course (2024 edition, likely materially similar for
2026 — confirm closer to date):**
- Water: ~mile 4, ~mile 5.5, mid-mile 9, and finish
- First aid: near miles 6 and 9-10, plus roaming cycle medics
- Oranges at St Christopher's Church, ~mile 7.5
- Toilets: Walpole Park (start/finish), St Peter's Church (mile 2), St
  Christopher's Church (miles 7-8)

(A separate, older/less-current source lists water at St Barnabas 4mi,
St Stephen's 6mi, St Mary's Hanwell — noted for completeness, but the
2024 Ealing.News breakdown above is the more recent, more specific
source.)

## 2. Conditions

**Climate normals for late September, London/Heathrow:** average high
~18°C, average low ~11°C, wind ~17.5 km/h (mostly westerly), rain
probability climbing through the month to ~26% by month's end. Humidity
is not typically a factor this time of year (dry autumn air).
([WeatherSpark Heathrow September
climate](https://weatherspark.com/m/147876/9/Average-Weather-in-September-at-London-Heathrow-Airport-United-Kingdom))

**What actually happened on recent race days**, per finisher reports —
more reliable for race-morning feel than climate averages:
- 2025: "dry... a bit warm wearing a t-shirt AND my charity vest" —
  warmed up more than expected as the race went on.
  ([Eat Cake and Run, 2025](https://eatcakeandrun.uk/2025/09/28/race-report-ealing-half-marathon-25/))
- 2024: "cold but dry, minimal wind" at the start; author notes she
  doesn't "remember it ever really raining in Ealing on the last Sunday
  of September" despite the ground being muddy from recent rain that
  week. ([Eat Cake and Run,
  2024](https://eatcakeandrun.uk/2024/09/29/race-report-ealing-half-marathon-2024/))
- An undated year: "perfect day — blue skies, cool but not cold" at the
  start, "a little warmer than expected" mid-race. ([Running on
  Full](https://runningonfullblog.com/race-review-ealing-half/))

**Pattern across reports:** cool/cold start (roughly 10-13°C at a 9am
September start), dry more often than not, but **warming noticeably by
the back half of the race** in more than one account — dress for the
start temperature but plan to shed a layer, don't overdress for the
whole race. Direct historical day-of records for Sept 27 specifically
were inconclusive in the sources I could pull (some conflicting
day-level signals in the raw archive); treat the pattern above as the
working assumption and re-check forecast in race week.

## 3. What finishers warn about

**The mile 10-11 wall, named independently by multiple sources across
different years:**
- "Mile 10 proved challenging for Mary (a first-time half marathoner)...
  transition to walk[ing] with some bits of running... that's when it
  sucks." ([Running on Full](https://runningonfullblog.com/race-review-ealing-half/))
- "The 11th mile" flagged as particularly hard, with a runner recalling
  "I could barely feel my legs" at that point. ([Redeemer London,
  2019](https://redeemerlondon.org/blog/2019/10/1/ealing-half-marathon-a-review))
- The 2025 finisher went down hard ("my toe caught something and I went
  flying") around 18km/~mile 11, after holding onto faster 2:15 pacers
  since roughly 16-17km — she frames it herself as overconfidence
  ("hubris will result in retribution") rather than a course hazard.
  ([Eat Cake and Run, 2025](https://eatcakeandrun.uk/2025/09/28/race-report-ealing-half-marathon-25/))

This isn't terrain-specific (the course isn't steep there) — it reads as
the classic late-race fade from going out too committed, amplified by
this race's specific character: **the crowd support is enormous** (30,000+
spectators, drumming, jelly babies, people out of their houses handing
out water) and multiple reports credit/blame it for carrying runners past
their real pace early. Favor these mid-pack, non-elite finisher accounts
over the official race recap, which only covers the winners
([UKRunChat official recap](https://www.ukrunchat.co.uk/ealing-half-marathon-race-report/)
— no course-feel content, times/prize-money only).

**Other things named directly by finishers:** sunscreen mattered even on
a "cool" day (one runner applied 30+ specifically); the doubling-back
sections mean real crowding at Uxbridge Road/Pitshanger Lane — don't
panic if pace bunches there, it's the course, not a fitness problem.

## 4. Gear and logistics

- **Start:** 9:00am half marathon (9:05am for the 5k), Walpole
  Park/Culmington Road area, Ealing W5/W13.
- **Bag drop:** Walpole Park race village, in the middle. Label (given
  with race number) attaches to the bag. Max bag size 40cm × 30cm ×
  10cm.
- **Parking:** No dedicated event parking. Two Ealing Council car parks
  within walking distance, payment required even on Sunday. Don't plan
  to park on roads near the village — closures start before the gun.
- **Transport:** Northfields (Piccadilly line, Heathrow branch) ~10 min
  walk; Ealing Broadway (Elizabeth line + mainline) ~15 min walk. Bus
  routes 65, 207/483, E2 run with race-day diversions.
- **Number/packet pickup:** In past years, early registrants got numbers
  by post; late registrants collected the Saturday before (10:30am-3pm)
  or race morning (7:15-8:45am) at the race village. Confirm the exact
  2026 cutoff and times closer to the date — this detail shifts year to
  year.
- **Gear call given the conditions above:** start-line layer you're
  comfortable ditching after a few miles (cool 10-13°C start, warms
  through the race per finisher reports); sunscreen even if it looks
  overcast; nothing technical/trail-specific needed — this is a 100%
  road course.

Sources: [ealinghalfmarathon.com](https://ealinghalfmarathon.com/),
[Ealing.News essential guide](https://www.ealing.news/sport/essential-guide-for-running-or-watching-the-ealing-half-marathon-2024/).

**Weather/heat — not currently a flagged limiter.** Nothing in Josh's

**Logistics — no injury-relevant exposure.** Straightforward road race,

## 7. What this means I need to train

1. **Extend the long run from 6mi toward an 11-12mi peak, on a strict
   ramp** — this is the #1 gap. No jump resembling the June injury's
   pattern; every increase stays inside the 10% cap per
   `athlete-profile.md`'s hard rule.
2. **Put 1-2 long runs on genuinely rolling terrain before race day** —
   low risk on this course specifically, but currently zero data exists
   on how Josh's knees/hips respond to any incline at all, and that's
   worth knowing before race morning, not on it.
3. **Hold true Z2 (124-144bpm) specifically on long runs, not just short
   easy runs** — the pacing-discipline risk at this race is real and
   named repeatedly by finishers; the fix is the same aerobic-efficiency
   work already underway, but it has to hold at mile 8-10 pace/effort,
   not just mile 1-3.
4. **Rehearse race-morning fueling on at least one long run at
   half-marathon-adjacent distance** before relying on the course's
   orange stop or personal gels for the first time on race day (per the
   Coaching Philosophy's David Roche fueling import).
5. **Plan the first 2 miles deliberately conservative, by HR not by
   adrenaline** — this course's specific, repeatedly-reported failure
   mode is getting swept out too fast by the crowd/pacers. Decide the
   opening pace range in advance, not on the start line.
