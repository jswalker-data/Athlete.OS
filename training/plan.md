# Training Plan — Ealing Half Marathon

_Built: 2026-07-20 | Race: Sunday 2026-09-27 | Target: 1:42:00 (4:50/km, 7:47/mi) | Built per the Coaching Philosophy in athlete-profile.md_

## Race demands (researched)

- **Distance:** 21.0975 km, single lap.
- **Course:** starts 9:00am at Walpole Park, Ealing, through Central Ealing, Montpelier, Pitshanger, Hanwell, and St Stephen's. Rolling, not flat — **~140m elevation gain** on one source, another puts it at ~95m; either way it's a genuine rolling course, not pancake-flat. ([13milers.com](https://13milers.com/races/ealing-half-marathon), [goodrunguide.co.uk](https://www.goodrunguide.co.uk/ElevationGraph.asp?RunType=S&RunID=299129))
- **Altitude:** sea level, London — no altitude factor.
- **Likely conditions:** late-September London, 9am start — historically ~11-14°C at the gun, rising toward 17-18°C by late morning, light-moderate wind (~10-11mph), a real but not high chance of rain. Cool is the operative word, not hot. ([AccuWeather](https://www.accuweather.com/en/gb/london/ec4a-2/september-weather/328328), [Weather Spark](https://weatherspark.com/m/45062/9/Average-Weather-in-September-in-London-United-Kingdom))
- **Implication:** this course rewards real hill strength, not flat speed-endurance alone, and heat/altitude are non-issues — cool, possibly damp is the training-and-racing reality to prepare for.

## Limiters, ranked

1. **Aerobic efficiency** — "easy" runs currently sit at 151-166bpm (Z3), not Z2 (124-144). This is the direct modern version of the "junk miles" pattern that broke you at uni. Fixing this is Phase 1's entire job.
2. **Long run ceiling** — current max ~9.7km vs. a 21.1km race. This is the single biggest gap, and the exact mechanism (one long run, done too soon) that caused the May injury — so it has to close on a strict ramp, not a leap.
3. **Consistency** — a chronic pattern across your full Garmin history (2017-2026), not just recent, and now partly explained by the May injury timeline above.
4. **No hill-specific training** — the course has real rolling terrain and you have zero hill-score data. Untrained for a demand the race actually has.
5. **Sleep irregularity** — roughly 1 in 4 recent nights under 5 hours. The one clearly low-readiness day in the data (Jul 19, score 34) was driven entirely by poor sleep, not training load.
6. **Known joint sensitivity** (knee/hip) — caps how fast any of the above can be closed, full stop.

## Weekly template

**Phase 1 (Bridge-Week 3):**
| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Strength AM (knee/hip, bodyweight — no gym) |
| Tue | Easy run, Z2 124-144bpm |
| Wed | Rest |
| Thu | Strength AM (AM only — your hard no on Thu evenings) |
| Fri | Rest |
| Sat | Parkrun — **controlled effort, not a time attempt** |
| Sun | Long run, Z2 124-144bpm |

Reasoning: 3 runs + 2 strength = 5 sessions, matches your comfortable range. Parkrun stays controlled through Phase 1 specifically because racing it hard would undercut the entire point of this phase — proving you can hold Z2 at pace.

**Phase 2-3 (Week 4-7):**
| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Strength AM |
| Tue | **Quality** — alternates threshold / hill week to week |
| Wed | Rest (optional easy shakeout if life allows — flex, not required) |
| Thu | Strength AM |
| Fri | Rest |
| Sat | Parkrun — effort ramps up, becomes a real fitness gauge |
| Sun | Long run, Z2 |

Reasoning: one quality session/week, alternating threshold and hill, rather than two — Bakken's hard-easy rhythm applied at your volume (his double-threshold model assumes far higher weekly mileage than you're building to; one quality day is the honest translation, not a diluted copy).

**Quality session menu:**
- **Threshold weeks:** 2×10min @ ~4:35-4:40/km, 2min jog recovery. Targets limiter #1 — pace-holding at race-relevant effort.
- **Hill weeks:** 6-8×60-90sec hard effort on the steepest local incline/stairs you can find (interim substitute until we solve the hills problem — see below), easy jog down. Targets limiter #4 directly, and it's the session you'll actually enjoy.

**Week 8 (taper):** same skeleton, Tuesday quality becomes 4×1km @ goal race pace (4:50/km), full recovery — van den Broek's rule that race-specific sharpening only belongs in the final 2 weeks lands exactly on this week. Saturday parkrun: easy jog only, no effort — protecting freshness into race week.

**Week 9 (race week):** Mon rest, Tue/Wed short easy shakeouts only, no long run, no Saturday parkrun effort (Sep 26 is the day before the race — jog or rest, not a time attempt). Race Sunday.

## Fueling

Start practicing race-day carb intake on long runs from Week 5 onward (first runs crossing ~75+ minutes): roughly **30-60g carbs/hour** (e.g. one gel every 40-45min). This is Roche's "train the gut, don't wing race day" principle, but the dose is recalibrated for your distance — his 120-150g/hr numbers are for multi-hour ultra efforts; at a 1:42 half you don't need or want that much, just a rehearsed, non-surprising race-day routine.

## Non-negotiable / adaptive rules

Carried directly from the Coaching Philosophy in athlete-profile.md, applied to this plan:

1. **Joint pain = stop, no negotiation.** Not soreness — pain.
2. **A missed or cut-short week is recovery, not regression.** Named explicitly so it doesn't trigger the "wasted week" spiral.
3. **Easy means Z2 (124-144bpm), even when the pace looks embarrassingly slow.** This is the fix, not a suggestion.
4. **10% cap on both weekly volume and long run — no exceptions without discussing it first.**
5. **Poor sleep/readiness that day → the session downgrades** (quality → easy, easy → rest), decided in the moment, not overridden by what the table says.
6. **No parkrun effort the week of the race.**
